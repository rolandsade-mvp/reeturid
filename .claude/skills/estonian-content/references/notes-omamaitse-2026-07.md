## Patterns worth stealing (food pieces)

### Recipe-step voice: 2nd-person singular imperative, verb first

Every Oma Maitse step opens with a bare sina-imperative and stays short:
"Pese sõstrad." · "Pane koos rootsudega keema." · "Keeda tasasel tulel
umbes tund aega, vahepeal sega." · "Tõsta moos ettevalmistatud
purkidesse ja sule õhukindlalt." · "Küpseta 200-kraadises ahjus 30
minutit, kuni pirukas on kuldpruun."

This matches Mealhive cook-mode voice exactly. The workhorse verbs:
pese, pane, keeda, sega, lisa, tõsta, aseta, määri, küpseta, rösti,
haki, nirista (drizzle), puista (sprinkle), riisu (skim), kaaneta (lid),
purusta, kurna (strain), lase seista (let rest).

### Concrete quantity idiom

- "näpuotsatäis soola" / "näpuotsaga tšillihelbeid" = a pinch
- "maitse järgi" = to taste; "vajadusel" = if needed
- sl / tl / dl abbreviations, decimal commas ("0,5 tl")
- Numeric ranges in recipe text use an EN dash: "2–3 minutit",
  "10–15 minutit". That is a "–", not the banned "—"; in running
  marketing prose the house rule (comma / kuni) still applies, but
  inside ingredient lists and step text the compact "2–3 min" form is
  the native convention.
- "tund aega" (a full hour, warmer than bare "1 tund"), "kümmekond
  minutit" (about ten), "tooni võrra tumedamaks" (a shade darker)
- Doneness by cue, not just clock: "kuni kuldpruun", "kuni marjad on
  pehmed", "kuni kaste on kokku keenud"

### Foreign dish/ingredient words: cite-form + apostrophe declension

Oma Maitse keeps the foreign spelling and declines with an apostrophe:
ricotta'ga, ricotta't, kimchi't, kimchi's, gochujang'i, dukkah'd,
hotteok'i, sushi'le, cheong'i. Italics for the foreign word where the
medium allows. When first introducing a term, gloss it with "ehk" or a
short appositive: "hapendatud ehk fermenteeritud", "bingsu ehk
bingsoo", "chikin ehk kana". Estonian term first, loanword as the
gloss, when a good Estonian word exists ("hapendamine" leads,
"fermenteerimine" explains).

### Headline and intro energy

- Direct questions to the reader: "Kas neid grillida oled proovinud?"
- Permission-to-be-done energy: "Aitab igavatest moosidest!"
- Label prefixes: "RETSEPT |", "Trendihoiatus:", "Hea teada:",
  "Soovitus!" (Mealhive equivalents: tip sheets, empty states, nudges)
- Warm, sensory adjectives that do real work: särtsakas (zingy), hõrk
  (delectable), krõbe / krõmpsuv (crispy / crunchy), mahlane, magushapu,
  suitsune, "puhas maitserõõm", "vitamiinipomm", "supermari"
- Seasonal-garden framing is the default emotional register of Estonian
  food media ("otse sinu koduaiast", "otse põõsast", picking berries
  "rootsudega") — Mealhive seasonal copy can lean on it.

### Useful vocabulary confirmations

- **hoidis** = preserve (the umbrella word; moos, želee, siirup under it)
- **külakost** = food you bring when visiting someone
- **lugejaretsept** = reader-submitted recipe
- **sauseguriga** = with an immersion blender
- "sobib nii pannkoogile kui liha juurde" — the "sobib nii X kui (ka) Y
  juurde" pattern for serving suggestions
- **portsjon(id)** is what recipe cards scale by ("PORTSJONEID: 4") —
  confirms the locked glossary's portsjon over taldrik for food amounts

### AI terminology (from the Ärileht/Economist piece)

Estonian mainstream media freely builds AI- hyphen compounds:
"AI-agent", "AI-intsident", alongside "tehisintellekt" spelled out and
"keelemudel" for a language model. This confirms the locked glossary's
"AI-salvestus" pattern — an "AI-" prefix compound reads as normal
Estonian, no need to reach for "tehisintellektipõhine ...".

## Second batch (2026-07-25): lifestyle + service journalism registers

Seven more Oma Maitse pieces: takeaway-coffee habits news, a party
organizer's tips interview, a celebrity-drinks taste test, a Lidl
sponsored plant-based piece (marked SISUTURUNDUS), two blueberry
dessert recipes, a Peipsi chef feature, and a Rimi
nutritionist's plant-grilling guide. New patterns worth having:

- **Expert-quote structure**: service pieces lead with a named expert
  ("terviseõde ja toitumisnõustaja Ly Juhe", "peokorraldaja Daire
  Kaup") and alternate short framing paragraphs with direct quotes.
  Mealhive blog equivalents can use the founder voice the same way.
- **Numbered practical-tip format**: the party piece is 1..6 numbered
  tips, each one imperative-led and concrete ("Mõtle läbi ka muusika
  tegemise võimalused"). Matches our how-to spokes.
- **Formula phrasing**: "Hea lihtne marinaad koosneb kolmest osast:
  rasv + hape + maitseained." Compact X + Y + Z formulas read
  naturally in ET service prose.
- **Warm diminutive dessert register**: "Mõnus magus amps!", "tibakese
  sidruniga", "väikese kookose". Good for celebratory/feature copy;
  too cute for UI strings.
- **Health-benefit framing vocabulary**: kiudained, täisväärtuslik,
  "jätab kõhtu kerge tunde", "rahakotisõbralik" (budget-friendly),
  "taskukohane". Useful for the food-waste/budget cluster.
- **"Vaja läheb:"** as the ingredient-list header in casual recipes
  (alongside the formal "Koostisosad").
- **Sponsored-content caution**: the Lidl piece (SISUTURUNDUS) shows
  the salesy end of the register ("Me ei tee üldse nalja!", "pööraselt
  soodne"). That hard-sell exclamation density is what Mealhive copy
  should stay UNDER; our register is the editorial pieces, not the ads.
- **Question-to-reader photo captions**: "Kas sina eelistad kohvitopsi
  kaasa haarata kohvikust või tanklast?" Direct sina-questions are
  normal engagement devices.

## What NOT to copy

- Portal chrome ("Kuula artiklit", reaction bars, SOOVITAME SULLE
  boxes) — layout noise, not voice.
- The two Ärileht business pieces (oil, Tesla) carry no food-copy value;
  they are neutral news register. If they were uploaded accidentally,
  they can be removed without loss.
- Oma Maitse uses "–" liberally in prose too; Mealhive's house rule
  still bans "—" everywhere and prefers comma/parentheses/kuni in
  marketing prose.
